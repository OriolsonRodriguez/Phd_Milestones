# Unofficial references — nothing here is cited

**Purpose.** A holding place for everything worth keeping that has no place in the research yet.
`references.bib` holds what is actually cited. This file holds the rest, for two different
reasons:

- **Unusable.** Not a primary source, not peer reviewed, advocacy or commentary rather than
  evidence, or journalism reporting a finding that should be cited from the paper itself. These
  will never enter `references.bib`. Sections: Journalism, Think tank and policy commentary, NGO
  and advocacy, Reference works and explainers.
- **Unused.** Sound peer-reviewed papers and primary documents that are interesting, possibly
  important later, but that the research does not draw on yet. Nothing is wrong with them; they
  simply have no home in the argument at present. Section: Sound but unused.

**Rules.**

1. Nothing in this file is cited anywhere. The only bibliography is `references.bib`.
2. This file is never added with `\addbibresource`. It is Markdown, not BibTeX, deliberately,
   so it cannot be compiled into a bibliography by accident.
3. An item moves to `references.bib` **when it is actually cited**, not because it is judged good.
   On moving, delete it from this file. No item lives in both.
4. Every entry carries a one-line note on what it is good for. An entry without that note is not
   worth keeping.
5. Keep the two categories apart. An unusable item that drifts into the unused section will
   eventually get cited by mistake.

**How to use it.** The unusable items are good for finding a primary source, dating an episode,
identifying which parties were involved, and seeing how an issue was framed publicly; once found,
cite the primary source. The unused items are the reading list: check it before starting a new
section, because something here may already answer the question.

## Sound but unused

citable in principle. They move to `references.bib` only when something in a document actually

  *Communications Earth & Environment*, 2025. <https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-025-02345-7>

## Journalism

Useful for naming parties the official record leaves anonymous, and for dating episodes.
Not citable: secondary reporting, no method, and the underlying facts are in the Final Reports.

- **China and Russia have again blocked moves to protect the endangered emperor penguin** — ABC

  Parties". Best single source for the attribution gap.

## Think tank and policy commentary

Informed, often by former negotiators, but opinion rather than evidence. Good for arguments and
counter-arguments to engage with; find the peer-reviewed equivalent before citing.

- **Soft Consensus, Hard Stalemates: Institutional Dilemma in Antarctic Governance** — Nong Hong,
  ICAS, 16 September 2025. <https://chinaus-icas.org/research/soft-consensus-hard-stalemates-institutional-dilemma-in-antarctic-governance/>
  The most useful single list of recently blocked proposals: East Antarctica, Weddell Sea and D1
  MPAs, Conservation Measure 51-07, the emperor penguin. Each needs verifying against the record.
- **The Continuing Value of Consensus-Based Decision-Making in the Antarctic Treaty System** —
  Evan T. Bloom, Wilson Center, 17 October 2024.
  <https://www.wilsoncenter.org/article/continuing-value-consensus-based-decision-making-antarctic-treaty-system>
  The counter-argument to the reformist premise, from a former US negotiator: consensus blocks
  conservation, and keeping it is still right. Worth engaging with wherever the motivation
  assumes deadlock is a failure.
- **Protecting the emperor penguin: what the Antarctic Treaty can actually do** — Jiliang Chen,
  Lowy Institute Interpreter, 25 May 2026.
  <https://www.lowyinstitute.org/the-interpreter/protecting-emperor-penguin-what-antarctic-treaty-can-actually-do>
  Records the objectors' mandate argument: the ATS lacks competence over climate change, which is
  the penguin's main threat. A procedural objection standing in for a substantive interest.
- **Antarctic Tourism After ATCM 48: From Visitor Management to Access Governance** — ICAS.
  <https://chinaus-icas.org/research/antarctic-tourism-after-atcm-48-from-visitor-management-to-access-governance/>
  Tourism as the clearest case of soft law substituting for binding regulation.
- **Tourism Governance In Antarctica: An Elusive Dream Even After ATCM-46** — Eurasia Review,
  4 June 2024. <https://www.eurasiareview.com/04062024-tourism-governance-in-antarctica-an-elusive-dream-even-after-atcm-46-analysis/>
  Notes the failed 1991 tourism annexe. Verify that against the record before using it.
- **Governing Antarctica in 2026** — CSIS. <https://www.csis.org/analysis/governing-antarctica-2026>
  Current geopolitical overview.
- **Antarctic Diplomacy in a BRICS+ World (No. 32)** — Wilson Center.
  <https://www.wilsoncenter.org/blog-post/no-32-antarctic-diplomacy-brics-world>
  Bloc behaviour among Consultative Parties. Suggestive for coalition work, not evidence for it.

  <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protocol_on_Environmental_Protection_to_the_Antarctic_Treaty>

  Liability). Cite the Protocol itself.

  <https://www.nti.org/education-center/treaties-and-regimes/antarctic-treaty/>

  <https://www.mofa.go.jp/press/release/pressite_000001_02360.html>

  Per-meeting sites carry logistics and document links; each meeting has its own.
- Search-engine filler encountered while researching, listed so it is not mistaken for a source

  LegalClarity <https://legalclarity.org/who-controls-antarctica-treaty-claims-and-governance/>.
  No rigour, no attribution. Do not use.

## Appendix: document access notes

Not references. Working notes on reaching the primary sources, kept here so they are not lost.

- ATCM Final Report: `https://documents.ats.aq/ATCM{N}/fr/ATCM{N}_fr001_e.pdf`
  (verified for N = 46, 47; no authentication needed)
- List of Documents for a meeting, giving WP number, agenda item, title and submitting parties
  as a table: `https://documents.ats.aq/ATCM47/WW/ATCM47_WW015_e.pdf`
  (the `WW` number varies by meeting, so it must be looked up)
- Working Paper: `https://documents.ats.aq/ATCM46/wp/ATCM46_wp034_e.docx`
  (note `.docx`, not `.doc`)
- `pdftotext -layout` parses both the Final Reports and the Lists of Documents into clean
  columns, so the tabled-versus-adopted frame can be built mechanically.
- The Rules of Procedure for both the ATCM and the CEP were revised in 2025 by Decision 2 (2025)
  and Decision 1 (2025), annexed to the ATCM 47 Final Report. The "Updated August 2023" edition
  is superseded.
- Final Reports carry the CEP report as an annex in the same PDF. The two layers attribute
  differently: the ATCM sections say "some Parties", the CEP annex names them. Search both.
- The Secretariat's list of Parties and their status, useful for dating accessions and
  Consultative Party status: <https://www.ats.aq/devAS/Parties?lang=e>
- Paywalled papers: ScienceDirect returns 403 to automated fetches. Open-access copies of British
  Antarctic Survey work are usually in NORA (<https://nora.nerc.ac.uk>), and PubMed Central
  (<https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov>) carries the biology. Both served full text where the publisher
  did not.
- The Secretariat document search at `ats.aq/devAS/Meetings/SearchDocDatabase` returned nothing
  useful to an automated fetch. The per-meeting List of Documents PDF is the reliable route.
